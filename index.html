Bio

Shayne Magstadt

I am a wildfire and geospatial researcher with a particular passion for artificial intelligence. I use machine learning, deep learning, computer vision, and large geospatial datasets to explore how AI can help us understand wildfire growth, evaluate suppression operations, interpret remote-sensing imagery, and support better fire-management decisions. My work brings together satellite and airborne imagery, aircraft-tracking data, fire-progression records, and other complex spatial information to develop practical AI-driven approaches to wildfire research.

My perspective is shaped by both technical research and fire-service experience. As a volunteer firefighter, I am especially interested in AI that is scientifically rigorous, transparent, and useful to people making decisions on the ground. Outside of research, I create glass art and handmade marbles through Woodlot Pyro Crafters, which gives me another way to explore complex patterns, color, and light.

Contact shayne.magstadt@colostate.edu

2026 Wildfire aviation

An assessment of aerial firefighting response times between agencies during the 2020 fire season in California

Open aircraft-tracking data were used to compare CAL FIRE and U.S. Forest Service aviation response across 7,214 California wildfires. The work shows how standardized ADS-B records can reveal cross-agency response patterns and support better resource coordination during initial attack.

2026 Geospatial AI

AI-Assisted Screening of Candidate Pond-Based Drafting Locations in a Rural Wildland-Urban Interface Fire Response Area

This study tests whether multimodal AI can scan high-resolution aerial imagery for ponds that appear accessible to fire apparatus. The workflow identified candidate water sources for local review while showing how AI screening can support field verification and firefighter judgment.

2026 Fire progression data

A high spatial resolution daily fire perimeter progression dataset for wildfires in the Western United States: 2020–2024

National Infrared Operations perimeters were organized into a consistent, reusable dataset that captures daily wildfire growth across the western United States. The resulting resource supports fire-behavior modeling, risk analysis, ecological assessment, and operational research.

2025 Fuel treatments

A neural network model to study factors impacting the selection of hazardous fuel treatment types in Colorado’s national forests

A neural network was trained on a decade of management records from 11 Colorado national forests to study how treatment types have been selected. Wildfire risk, expected fire behavior, and proximity to structures emerged as important influences across six treatment categories.

2024 Deep learning

A Deep Learning Approach for Predicting Aerial Suppressant Drops in Wildland Firefighting Using ADS-B Data

Aircraft trajectories from ADS-B were paired with verified suppressant-drop records to train sequential deep-learning models. The best model identified drop events with an F1 score of 0.922, demonstrating a path toward more complete, near-real-time awareness of aerial suppression activity.

2024 Wildfire workforce

Retention of highly qualified wildland firefighters in the Western United States

Employment histories for Interagency Hotshot Crew members were analyzed to understand why experienced firefighters stay or leave. Greater workload and cumulative experience were associated with stronger retention, while wages in alternative occupations were not a significant predictor.

2021 UAV remote sensing

Can a Remote Sensing Approach with Hyperspectral Data Provide Early Detection and Mapping of Black Bear Bark Stripping in Coast Redwoods?

UAV hyperspectral imagery was evaluated as a way to find bear-damaged redwoods before the damage becomes obvious from the air. Healthy trees could be separated from trees with older damage, while very recent damage remained difficult to distinguish, helping define the next steps for earlier detection.

2020 Coastal landscapes

Factors Influencing Movement of the Manila Dunes and Its Impact on Establishing Non-Native Species

Fine-scale UAV imagery and photogrammetry were used to measure dune movement and map vegetation at California’s Manila Dunes. The study connected dune stability with trails, topography, and plant cover, providing a baseline for managing recreation and invasive species in a dynamic coastal system.
